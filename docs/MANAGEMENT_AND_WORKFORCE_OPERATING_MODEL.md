# ASAA Management and Workforce Operating Model

**Status:** Proposed operating design for owner validation  
**Applies to:** ASAA's future internal organization and workforce  
**Relationship:** Expands the enterprise operating model and organization hierarchy; does not claim implementation  
**Technology policy:** Technology-stack independent

## 1. Purpose and completion status

This document defines how ASAA should manage the company and its employees from launch through growth. It joins leadership, department ownership, people management, employee lifecycle, workforce operations, performance, employee support and management reporting into one operating model.

The existing repository already contains a proposed hierarchy, a broad department catalogue, role/access concepts and initial HR, attendance, leave, work-log and payroll stories. Those are a useful foundation, but they do not yet constitute a fully validated management system. This document closes the design gap at the process and responsibility level.

**Current status**
- Management capability catalogue: defined.
- Proposed hierarchy and role families: defined.
- Launch leadership appointments and reporting lines: pending owner confirmation.
- Actual departments and department heads: pending business activation.
- HR policies, employment terms, work schedules, leave, compensation and approval limits: pending owner approval and applicable professional/legal review.
- Application implementation and operational verification: not started / not evidenced by this document.

## 2. Management design principles

1. One accountable owner is identified for every organizational unit, process and material decision.
2. Authority comes from an explicit, current delegation or permission—not merely a job title.
3. Reporting management, functional supervision, project coordination and approval authority are separate relationships.
4. A small team may combine roles; the model must support separation of duties as the organization grows.
5. Employee records and management decisions are effective-dated and historically traceable.
6. Managers receive the minimum employee information needed for their responsibilities.
7. Employee performance is assessed using agreed outcomes, role expectations, quality, collaboration and context—not attendance or raw activity alone.
8. Policies are versioned, approved and effective-dated; no employment or statutory rule is assumed.
9. Employees have a fair route to understand expectations, raise concerns, request corrections and appeal or seek review where policy provides.
10. The same management model supports office, remote, hybrid, field, shift, project-based and other approved work patterns.
11. Every management dashboard identifies its data source, definition, period, freshness and access scope.
12. Automation may assist reminders, routing and analysis but may not independently make sensitive employment decisions.

## 3. Company leadership and management hierarchy

### 3.1 Conceptual hierarchy

```text
Organization
└── Founder / Organization Owner
    ├── Board / Advisors (only if established)
    └── CEO / Managing Director (may be the Founder/Owner)
        ├── Executive / Functional Leaders
        ├── Department Heads
        │   └── Department Managers
        │       └── Team Leads / Supervisors
        │           └── Employees / Specialists / Operations Staff
        └── Shared Services / Central Functions
```

This is a configurable pattern, not a mandatory number of layers. At launch, one person may hold several responsibilities and some departments may be inactive. As staffing grows, responsibilities can be separated and leadership appointments made through recorded decisions.

### 3.2 Leadership responsibilities

| Role | Core accountability | Typical decisions | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder | Establishes the organization, vision and founding intent | Founding direction and initial appointments | Does not automatically replace legal authority or required independent controls |
| Organization Owner | Ultimate internal owner of organizational configuration and delegated authority | Organization structure, appointments, policy ownership and reserved decisions | Sensitive personal records remain subject to purpose, privacy and access controls |
| CEO / Managing Director | Executes approved strategy and leads company operations | Cross-company priorities, operating plans and executive coordination | Acts within authority formally delegated by the owner and any applicable governance |
| Executive / Functional Leader | Outcomes across an assigned function or portfolio | Functional strategy, resources and cross-department decisions | Authority limited to assigned scope and delegation |
| Department Head | Department results, people, process, budget and controls | Department plans, assignments and permitted approvals | Cannot approve outside scope or access restricted records by title alone |
| Department Manager | Daily execution, staffing coordination, work quality and escalations | Work allocation, coaching and routine operational approvals | Acts within manager delegation and employee privacy rules |
| Team Lead / Supervisor | Daily team coordination, support and first-line quality | Task coordination, review and operational escalation | Does not automatically gain compensation, disciplinary or HR authority |
| Employee / Contributor | Performs agreed responsibilities and maintains required work records | Routine decisions within assigned work | No managerial authority unless separately delegated |

The Founder, Owner and CEO may be one person or separate people. This is a required owner decision, not a fact to infer from this document. Any board, statutory officer or legally defined authority is activated only when applicable and confirmed.

### 3.3 Reserved and delegated decisions

The owner should maintain a decision and delegation register. Each delegation records:
- Delegating authority and delegate.
- Decision/action type and organizational scope.
- Monetary or risk limit, only when approved.
- Effective date, expiry/review date and revocation status.
- Conditions, required reviewers and prohibited self-approval.
- Evidence, rationale and audit history.

Reserved matters should be explicitly listed by the owner. Until approved, ASAA must not infer that a CEO, department head or manager can make a high-impact decision simply because of their title.

## 4. Department operating model

Every activated department must have a department charter. A charter contains:
- Name, purpose, scope and services provided.
- Parent business unit and effective status.
- Accountable department head/owner and acting delegate.
- Responsibilities, exclusions and interfaces with other departments.
- Team structure, positions and approved staffing plan.
- Annual/periodic objectives and measurable service or outcome indicators.
- Budget/cost-centre references when finance is activated.
- Processes, policies, risks, controls and required records.
- Approval authority and escalation route.
- Required skills, training and access profiles.
- Dependencies, internal customers and external obligations.
- Review cadence and change history.

Department states: Planned, Active, Temporarily Suspended, Inactive, Archived. Deactivation must preserve historical employee assignments, records, approvals and reporting.

### 4.1 Department head responsibilities

A department head is accountable for:
1. Translating company strategy into a department plan.
2. Maintaining the department charter, services and process ownership.
3. Proposing staffing and capability requirements.
4. Assigning managers and clarifying decision boundaries.
5. Setting team objectives aligned to company priorities.
6. Reviewing delivery, quality, risk, capacity and service levels.
7. Managing approved budgets and resources within delegated limits.
8. Ensuring policies, training, controls and records are maintained.
9. Resolving escalations or routing them to the correct authority.
10. Reporting material exceptions and proposing corrective actions.

### 4.2 Manager and team-lead responsibilities

Managers translate department objectives into work plans, assign work fairly, coach employees, review deliverables, monitor workload and availability, handle routine approvals within delegation, document material feedback and escalate issues. Team leads coordinate daily execution and provide first-line support; they do not automatically become formal HR managers.

### 4.3 Cross-functional work

Every cross-functional initiative must identify:
- One accountable initiative/project owner.
- Contributing departments and named workstream owners.
- Deliverables, dependencies, target dates and acceptance authority.
- Resource commitments and conflict-resolution route.
- Separate HR reporting manager and delivery coordinator.
- Decision and change history.

A project manager may coordinate an employee's project work without changing their formal reporting line or receiving unrestricted employee-file access.

## 5. Employee lifecycle management

ASAA must maintain one person identity and a linked, controlled employment/engagement lifecycle.

### 5.1 Lifecycle stages

1. Workforce need identified and approved.
2. Position or engagement request created.
3. Recruitment / sourcing / selection (if applicable).
4. Offer or engagement terms approved.
5. Pre-joining checks and documentation completed where lawful and applicable.
6. Onboarding plan and accountable manager assigned.
7. Active employment/engagement.
8. Probation or initial review where contract/policy provides.
9. Ongoing role, performance, learning and employee support.
10. Change event: transfer, promotion, manager change, work-mode change, compensation change or contract amendment.
11. Leave, temporary suspension or other approved status where applicable.
12. Exit initiated, reviewed and approved.
13. Handover, asset return, access revocation and final records completed.
14. Former-employee record retained, restricted, exported or lawfully disposed under approved retention rules.

Each transition must record effective date, initiator, approver, reason, supporting documents, affected assignments, notifications and audit event. A workflow may be adapted for contractors, interns, freelancers and other engagement types; do not treat every person as an employee.

### 5.2 Employee master profile

The employee/person record should link, rather than duplicate:
- Identity and permitted contact details.
- Employment/engagement agreement and status.
- Department, team, position, job family and grade if used.
- Primary manager and any secondary/matrix relationships.
- Work location, work pattern and calendar.
- Skills, qualifications and training records.
- Assigned assets and access memberships.
- Attendance, leave, timesheets and approved work records.
- Goals, review outcomes and development plans.
- Compensation and payroll references in a separately restricted domain.
- Employee requests, acknowledgements and case references with restricted access.
- Change history and retention classification.

Sensitive identity, bank, health, compensation, grievance and disciplinary information must be separately classified and permissioned. A manager's general team view must not expose those fields by default.

## 6. Core employee-management processes

### 6.1 Onboarding

A configurable onboarding plan should cover:
- Approved role, manager, department, start date and work arrangement.
- Contract/engagement documents and required acknowledgements.
- Identity and eligibility checks only where lawful and necessary.
- Equipment/assets and custody acknowledgement.
- Account and system access requests with least privilege.
- Role-specific induction, policies and required training.
- Initial objectives, responsibilities and success expectations.
- Buddy/mentor assignment where used.
- Completion evidence, outstanding tasks and escalation.

Access is granted only after required approvals and must be time-bounded where appropriate. HR onboarding completion must not imply every system permission is granted.

### 6.2 Work planning and assignment

Managers must be able to:
- View team capacity and approved availability within their scope.
- Assign work with owner, outcome, priority, due date and dependencies.
- Balance workload across employees and projects.
- Record reassignment and reason.
- Identify blocked, overdue, unowned and over-capacity work.
- Distinguish planned work, urgent work, operational duties and development time.
- Respect approved work patterns, leave, accommodations and access restrictions.

Attendance duration, online presence, keystrokes or task counts must not be treated as standalone productivity measures.

### 6.3 Attendance, leave and work schedules

The platform supports configurable work calendars, shifts, check-in/out, breaks, corrections, leave requests, approvals, holidays, overtime and availability. The actual rules remain pending approved policy and applicable jurisdiction. Employees can inspect their own records and request correction; managers can review operational exceptions within scope; HR/policy owners administer rules and exceptions.

Location/device verification must be proportionate, transparent and explicitly approved. No covert tracking or continuous surveillance is part of this model.

### 6.4 Work logs and timesheets

Employees record work outcomes, blockers and relevant project/activity references according to approved process. Managers review completeness and work context; project approvers validate project effort; finance may validate billable classifications. Attendance and work-log duration remain separate. Timesheet approval is not a substitute for performance review.

### 6.5 Goals and performance management

The performance cycle should be configurable and include:
1. Role expectations and measurable outcomes agreed at the start of a cycle.
2. Individual goals aligned to department/company objectives.
3. Periodic check-ins and feedback, not only annual review.
4. Employee self-reflection and evidence.
5. Manager assessment with examples, context and documented rationale.
6. Optional peer/stakeholder feedback with safeguards against popularity scoring.
7. Review discussion and employee response.
8. Development actions, support and next-cycle goals.
9. Calibration or independent review only if the company adopts it.
10. A documented correction, appeal or second-review route under approved policy.

Assessment dimensions may include outcomes, quality, role competence, reliability, collaboration, customer impact, initiative, learning and leadership where relevant to the role. Criteria must be role-appropriate and disclosed. Do not use opaque AI scores, unvalidated rankings or attendance alone to determine performance, promotion, compensation or termination.

### 6.6 Learning, skills and career development

Maintain role skill profiles, required qualifications, learning plans, training enrolments, completion evidence, certification expiry and career interests where appropriate. Managers identify capability gaps and support development; employees can propose goals and review their records. Access to personal development notes must be limited to the employee and authorized reviewers.

### 6.7 Employee support, feedback and workplace cases

Provide distinct workflows for:
- General HR questions and service requests.
- Policy clarification and corrections.
- Workplace concerns, grievances and complaints.
- Conflict-of-interest disclosures.
- Safety or wellbeing concerns where applicable.
- Formal conduct/disciplinary matters only under approved policy and appropriate professional/legal review.

Confidential cases require restricted case teams, conflict checks, evidence integrity, impartial review where feasible, response timelines, employee communication and a controlled outcome/appeal record. A line manager must not automatically access or decide a case involving themselves. Do not create an informal blacklisting or secret employee rating.

### 6.8 Compensation, payroll and employee benefits

Maintain restricted, effective-dated compensation and benefits records. Separate preparation, review, approval and payment execution. Payroll, statutory deductions, tax treatment, payslip format, benefits and final settlement remain gated by confirmed legal jurisdiction, company status, approved employment terms and professional review. No salary values or statutory rules are assumed.

### 6.9 Transfers, promotions and exits

Every role/manager/department/compensation change must be approved under the relevant authority, effective-dated and reflected across access, reporting, cost allocation and assigned work. Exit workflow covers reason category with restricted access, notice/contract steps where applicable, handover, assets, account revocation, final settlement inputs, employee documents, records retention and post-exit access review.

## 7. Management cadence and communication

The platform should support configurable operating rhythms, not impose calendar frequencies:
- Company leadership review: strategy, objectives, finance, risks, workforce and decisions.
- Department review: objectives, delivery, capacity, quality, budget and escalations.
- Team planning/review: work allocation, blockers, dependencies and support.
- Employee check-ins: progress, feedback, wellbeing/workload concerns and development.
- Performance cycle: agreed review windows and documented outcomes.
- Policy and access review: scheduled or event-triggered review.
- Incident and exception review: prompt handling based on severity.

Every meeting or review may create decisions and action items with owner, due date, status, evidence and escalation. Announcements and policies must target relevant audiences and record acknowledgements when required.

## 8. Management dashboards

### Owner / CEO view
- Organization structure and leadership ownership gaps.
- Company objectives and strategic initiatives.
- Department status, material risks and unresolved decisions.
- Workforce headcount by approved categories, vacancies and capacity.
- Attendance/leave exceptions only at an appropriate summary level.
- Delivery, service, customer and financial indicators.
- Approvals awaiting action and overdue commitments.
- Data freshness, confidence and drill-down permissions.

### Department head view
- Department objectives and outcomes.
- Staffing, vacancies, capacity and workload.
- Delivery commitments, quality and service levels.
- Attendance/leave availability summaries.
- Training/certification requirements and capability gaps.
- Budget/resource position when activated.
- Risks, policy exceptions, approvals and escalations.

### Manager / team-lead view
- Direct-report and assigned-team roster.
- Work assignments, priorities, due dates and blockers.
- Team availability and schedule exceptions.
- Work review and approval queue.
- Check-in and development actions.
- Restricted employee information only when explicitly authorized.

### Employee view
- Personal profile and assigned role expectations.
- Work queue, priorities, due dates and relevant team updates.
- Own attendance, leave, shifts and work logs.
- Own goals, feedback, training and development plan.
- Requests, approvals, acknowledgements and documents.
- Correction and support channels.

Dashboards must not rank employees using hidden formulas or expose peer compensation or confidential cases.

## 9. Responsibility and access matrix

| Capability | Owner / CEO | Department Head | Manager / Lead | HR | Employee |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organization structure | Approve / administer per delegation | Propose changes | View relevant scope | Administer assigned records | View relevant information |
| Department charter | Approve / review | Own | Contribute / execute | Support role records | View relevant responsibilities |
| Employee profile | Restricted oversight by explicit grant | Limited work-relevant view | Limited direct-report view | Administer assigned fields | View own profile |
| Work assignment | Enterprise priorities | Department allocation | Team allocation | No routine task authority | Perform assigned work |
| Attendance / leave | Policy oversight, not unrestricted detail | Team summaries / delegated decisions | Team review / delegated approvals | Policy administration | Own records / requests |
| Performance | Governance / senior appointments | Department review | Direct-report review | Process and fairness support | Self-review / response |
| Compensation / payroll | Explicitly authorized oversight | No default access | No default access | Restricted preparation | Own approved records |
| Grievance / conduct case | Only authorized independent role | No automatic access | No access if conflicted | Restricted case handler | Submit / respond / appeal |
| Access grants | Reserved/admin authority as approved | Request role access | Request team access | Request HR access | No self-grant |
| Exit | Approve per reserved authority | Initiate / handover | Support handover | Coordinate controlled process | Complete assigned steps |

This is a responsibility baseline, not a final permission grant. Exact permissions, approval steps and sensitive-field scopes must be configured and approved.

## 10. Required management records

At minimum, design for:
- Leadership appointment and delegation.
- Department charter and department ownership.
- Position, vacancy and assignment.
- Reporting relationship and history.
- Person/employee and engagement record.
- Onboarding/offboarding checklist and evidence.
- Work plan, assignment and capacity allocation.
- Attendance event, correction, leave request and schedule.
- Work log and timesheet approval.
- Goal, check-in, feedback and performance review.
- Skill, training and certification.
- Employee request and confidential case.
- Compensation/benefit change and payroll review.
- Management meeting, decision and action item.
- Approval, exception, escalation and audit event.

Every record needs an owner, lifecycle, access classification, retention rule, effective date where applicable and traceable correction history.

## 11. Management control and exception rules

- No self-approval for actions prohibited by the configured separation-of-duties policy.
- Temporary delegation must expire or be reviewed.
- A manager change must trigger access re-evaluation.
- Transfer/offboarding must trigger access, asset, assignment and reporting reconciliation.
- Missing attendance or work records are exceptions to resolve, not proof of misconduct.
- Performance concerns require disclosed criteria, evidence, employee context and a review route.
- Sensitive employee cases require conflict-of-interest handling and restricted access.
- Manager dashboards aggregate sensitive data only to the minimum necessary level.
- Employee records cannot be silently rewritten; corrections retain actor, reason and history.
- AI can summarize evidence or suggest next steps but cannot autonomously hire, reject, rank, discipline, compensate or terminate employees.
- Emergency access is time-bound, justified, logged and reviewed.

## 12. Rollout sequence

### Management Foundation 1 — Governance and organization
- Confirm Founder / Owner / CEO arrangement.
- Approve reserved decisions and delegation model.
- Activate only the launch departments and assign accountable owners.
- Approve department charter template, reporting relationships and role/permission boundaries.

### Management Foundation 2 — Employee records and access
- Define person/engagement categories and minimum employee profile.
- Approve employee-data classification, access and retention.
- Define position/assignment and manager history.
- Validate onboarding, transfer and offboarding process maps.

### Workforce Release 1 — Daily operations
- Onboarding, employee directory, work allocation and team availability.
- Attendance, leave, shifts and work logs under approved policies.
- Employee self-service, manager queues and HR administration.

### Workforce Release 2 — People development and support
- Goals, check-ins, performance review and development plans.
- Skills, training, certification and employee feedback.
- Confidential HR cases and controlled escalation.

### Workforce Release 3 — Compensation and advanced operations
- Compensation, benefits and payroll inputs after legal/policy gates.
- Workforce planning, recruitment and capacity analytics.
- Advanced management dashboards and approved automation.

## 14. Acceptance criteria for the management model

The management model is ready for implementation planning when:
- Launch leadership and reserved/delegated authority are owner-confirmed.
- Each active department has an approved charter, owner and escalation path.
- Reporting, functional supervision, project coordination and approval authority are distinct.
- Employee lifecycle states and transition owners are agreed.
- Employee-data classification, access and retention are approved.
- Onboarding, work allocation, attendance, leave, work logs, performance, employee support and exit have process maps and testable criteria.
- Employee and manager self-service journeys are defined.
- Dashboards and KPIs have approved definitions and permissions.
- Sensitive employment decisions have human review, conflict controls and appeal/correction paths.
- Applicable legal/policy gates are resolved before the related workflow is activated.
- No feature is marked implemented without test evidence and operational ownership.

**Conclusion:** The main management framework is now specified as a proposed operating design. The actual launch appointments, active departments, authority limits and HR policies still require owner confirmation. The application remains unimplemented unless separate code and test evidence establishes otherwise.
